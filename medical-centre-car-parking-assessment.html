Parking for a new clinic

Medical centres often need a car parking assessment. Many open in existing shops or houses with little on-site parking, and most patients arrive by car for short appointments throughout the day.

A car parking demand assessment shows council how many spaces the clinic will really need, based on how many practitioners consult at once and how long appointments run. It gives council the evidence to approve the parking you can provide.

How Clause 52.06 sets medical centre parking

Clause 52.06 is the part of every Victorian planning scheme that sets how many car parking spaces a use needs. For a medical centre, the number is based on leasable floor area: the floor space of the clinic itself, not shared areas such as malls or public toilets. A veterinary centre uses the same rate. Since Amendment VC277, it also depends on the site's parking category:

  • Category 1: 5 spaces to each 100 square metres of leasable floor area
  • Category 2: 3.5 spaces to each 100 square metres
  • Category 3: no minimum, and a maximum of 5 spaces to each 100 square metres
  • Category 4: a maximum of 5 spaces to each 100 square metres

To find your site's category, look up the address in VicPlan, the state government's free planning map, and turn on the Car Parking Requirement layer. Category 1 asks for the most parking, usually where public transport is limited. Category 4 asks for the least, usually near frequent train, tram or bus services.

The rate follows floor area, but demand follows how many practitioners consult at once and how long appointments run. A small practice in a large tenancy may need fewer spaces than the rate suggests, and a busy one in a small tenancy may need more. A demand assessment tests which applies.

A Parking Overlay can set a different rate, so we check the planning scheme for the site first. Our guide explains the VC277 parking categories and when you need a permit to reduce parking.

What council looks for at a medical centre

  • Number of practitioners consulting at the same time
  • Appointment length, and how quickly spaces turn over
  • Reception and support staff parking
  • Opening hours, including evenings and weekends
  • Accessible parking for patients with limited mobility
  • On-street parking near the site, and its time limits

Council may limit the number of practitioners through a permit condition, so it's worth settling the number you need at the start.

How we assess a medical centre

We start with how the clinic will run: the number of consulting rooms and practitioners, appointment lengths, staff numbers and opening hours. Then we estimate demand at the peak and test it against the spaces available.

  • Peak demand from practitioners, staff and patients
  • Surveys of comparable clinics where needed
  • Sharing spaces with nearby uses that peak at other times
  • On-street parking supply and occupancy
  • Layout checked against the Australian Standards for parking (AS 2890.1 and AS 2890.6)
  • Patient drop-off near the entrance

When you might not need a parking permit

Not every parking shortfall needs a planning permit. Under Clause 52.06-3, you may not need one if:

  • The new use needs no more parking than the current use, and no existing spaces are removed
  • The site is in a commercial zone, such as a shopping strip, the use goes into an existing building without adding floor area, and the shortfall is 10 spaces or fewer

Some conditions apply. For example, the commercial zone exemption doesn't apply where a Parking Overlay lets you pay instead of providing spaces. If you do need a permit for a shortfall of 10 spaces or fewer, it can go through VicSmart, the state's faster permit process. If parking is the only permit you need, the application doesn't go out for public notice. We'll check which applies before you lodge.

Turning a shop or house into a clinic

Where a clinic moves into an existing building, the assessment can take account of any parking shortfall already associated with the current use. That can narrow the gap council is asked to accept.

Frequently asked questions

How many car parks does a medical centre need in Victoria?

It depends on the site's Car Parking Requirement map category. Under Clause 52.06, the rate is 5 spaces to each 100 square metres of leasable floor area in Category 1 and 3.5 in Category 2. Categories 3 and 4 have no minimum and a maximum of 5. A Parking Overlay can set a different rate.

Can I open a clinic in a shop with no parking?

Often. In a commercial zone, a clinic moving into an existing building doesn't need a permit for the parking shortfall if it's 10 spaces or fewer and the floor area doesn't increase. Otherwise you'll need a permit to reduce the requirement under Clause 52.06-3, and council will want a car parking demand assessment showing where practitioners, staff and patients will park.

Does a dental or physio clinic count as a medical centre?

Usually. Clinics that treat out-patients, including dental, physiotherapy and other allied health practices, generally fall under the medical centre use term. A veterinary centre is a separate use, but Clause 52.06 sets the same parking rate.

Will council limit the number of practitioners?

It may. Council often ties the permit to a maximum number of practitioners, based on the parking assessment. We base the assessment on the number you plan to have, so the permit fits how you'll operate.

How long does it take?

Usually one to two weeks once plans and the brief are confirmed. It runs longer where surveys are needed, since those have to be done on representative days and avoid school and public holidays.
