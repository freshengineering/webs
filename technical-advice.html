Road safety barrier design advice

Barrier advice lives in the detail. We work to the Austroads Guide to Road Design Part 6: Roadside Design, Safety and Barriers, and AS/NZS 3845. We check the system's working width and deflection against design speed and geometry. Then we confirm the hazard sits clear of the space the barrier moves into when it is hit. Get the offset wrong and the barrier won't protect what it's there for.

Our team is ASHTAS certified, under the Austroads Safety Hardware Training and Accreditation Scheme.

  • Containment and MASH test level selection
  • Working width and dynamic deflection checks
  • Barrier products accepted by DTP (RDN 06-04) and Austroads
  • Offset to hazard and clearance behind the rail
  • Terminals, transitions and connection details

Constructability and live-road staging reviews

Building on a live road is where good plans come unstuck. We sit in the workshop and mark up the staging with the contractor. Then we check it against sight distance, merges and the realities of getting plant in and out. The aim is a sequence that's safe, buildable and won't stall at design review. At tender, the same work becomes the staging methodology your price is built on.

  • Sequencing and constructability on live carriageways
  • Temporary barrier and worker protection, tied into the temporary traffic management design
  • Sight distance and merge geometry
  • Occupancy and changeover risk
  • Resolving conflicts between standards and site

Frequently asked questions

When do I need technical advice rather than a standard design?

When the answer isn't in the standard drawings, like a barrier that can't make its deflection in the available offset or staging on a live high-speed road. Also when a DTP or council reviewer has raised something you need a defensible position on.

How do you assess barrier deflection?

We check the system's working width and dynamic deflection against design speed, vehicle type and geometry, to confirm the hazard or worker sits outside the deflection envelope. It's the first thing a reviewer looks at, so we get it right early.

How fast can you turn it around?

A focused question, like confirming a barrier offset, is often a few days. A complex constructability problem with options and calculations usually runs one to two weeks, and we'll prioritise where the program demands it.

Can you help respond to road safety audit findings?

Yes, for audits carried out by other auditors. When a road safety audit comes back with findings the design team disputes or can't easily resolve, we prepare the engineering position for the response table. It covers what the finding means, what the options are, and the reasons if a recommendation isn't adopted. A response holds more weight when the reasoning is documented properly. We never prepare responses to our own audits, so each audit stays independent.

How much does technical advice cost?

It tracks the size of the question. A quick check of a barrier offset or a marked-up plan is a small, fast job. A structured advice note with options and calculations for a staging or constructability problem takes longer. Describe the problem and we'll scope it, usually as a fixed fee.
