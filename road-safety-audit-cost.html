Road safety audit cost in Victoria: the short answer

There's no flat fee for a road safety audit (RSA). The price depends mainly on two things: how big the project is, and which stage it is at.

A detailed design audit of one intersection is a much smaller job than a pre-opening audit of a major road upgrade with day and night inspections.

What affects the cost of a road safety audit

Here is how each factor changes the size of the job.

  • Audit stage: feasibility, preliminary design and detailed design audits are mainly a desk-based review of the drawings, plus a site visit to see the existing conditions. A pre-opening audit includes a day inspection and a night inspection of the finished works, so it needs more time on site. Post-opening and existing roads audits centre on a site inspection, and existing roads audits also review the crash history. Roadworks and temporary traffic management audits review the setup on the road. They also review the traffic management plan (TMP) or traffic guidance scheme (TGS, the sign and cone layout drawings).
  • Project size and complexity: the length of road, the number of intersections, and whether there are interchanges, signals, safety barriers, or walking and cycling facilities. Each adds drawings to review and locations to inspect.
  • Number of stages: if your project needs more than one stage, such as detailed design and pre-opening, it can be more efficient to scope them together up front. The audit team learns the project once, and later stages build on earlier findings.
  • Site location and travel: sites in metropolitan Melbourne need less travel time than sites in regional Victoria. Travel matters most where both day and night inspections are needed.
  • Information supplied: a complete, current design package lets the audit run efficiently. Missing drawings, or drawings that change during the audit, mean extra requests and extra review.
  • The audit team: Austroads Part 6 says an audit is done by a team independent of the design. In Victoria, the Department of Transport and Planning (DTP) accredits road safety auditors (read about our accredited road safety auditors). Larger or more specialised projects may need a bigger team, or specialist input such as safety barrier advice.
  • Road authority requirements: if DTP or a council will review the report, their requirements for the audit team, report format or close-out can add to the scope.
  • Timeframe: a tight deadline, or a pre-opening inspection booked at short notice, can affect the scope and price. Tell us your dates early.
  • Response and close-out support: after the report is issued, the client and designer respond to each finding. Some projects also need the auditor to review those responses, attend a close-out meeting or comment on revised drawings. Let us know if you expect this, so it can be included.

What a road safety audit quote usually includes

A clear quote tells you exactly what you're paying for. Ours set out:

  • The scope: the project, the extent of works and the drawings to be audited
  • The audit stage or stages covered
  • Site inspections, including the night inspection for a pre-opening audit
  • The audit team, led by an accredited Senior Road Safety Auditor
  • An audit report with risk-rated findings by location, practical recommendations, annotated plans and site photos
  • A response table so each finding can be closed out
  • Close-out support, if your project needs it

It's worth checking what isn't included too, such as re-auditing revised drawings or extra inspections. A clear scope means no surprises later.

Why the cheapest audit can cost more

It's sensible to compare quotes. Compare the scope as well as the price.

An audit that misses a finding doesn't remove the risk. It moves it to a later stage, where it costs more to deal with. A finding at preliminary design is a change to a drawing. The same finding at pre-opening can mean rework, delay and cost.

When comparing quotes, check:

  • Are the auditors on the Victorian Road Safety Auditors Register, at the level the road authority requires?
  • Is the audit team independent of the design team?
  • Does a pre-opening quote include the night inspection?
  • Is review of the responses and close-out covered, or extra?
  • Will the report give the designer clear, buildable recommendations?

The earlier you bring the auditor in, the cheaper the findings are to act on.

Frequently asked questions

Do you give fixed-price quotes?

Yes. Send us the drawings and scope and we'll quote a fixed fee and timing. The quote sets out what's included, so you know what you're paying for before the audit starts.

Is a night inspection always needed?

No. A night inspection is standard practice for a pre-opening audit under Austroads Part 6, so lighting, delineation and signs can be checked in the dark. Design-stage audits don't usually need one. For existing roads and roadworks audits, it depends on the issues, such as night-time crashes or works carried out at night.

Can I bundle several audit stages into one quote?

Yes. If you know your project will need more than one stage, such as detailed design and pre-opening, we can scope them together. Booking early also helps plan the pre-opening day and night inspections around your opening date. Each stage is still its own audit with its own report.

Do you charge for travel to regional Victoria?

We work across Melbourne and regional Victoria. Site location is one of the things we factor into the quote, so tell us where the site is when you ask.

Does the audit fee include fixing the findings?

No. The audit identifies risks and recommends treatments. The client and designer decide how to respond, and any design changes or construction work are separate. The auditors don't redesign the project, so the audit stays independent.
