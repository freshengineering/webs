Traffic management plans and construction traffic in Victoria

A traffic management plan (TMP) sets out how road users, pedestrians, cyclists and workers stay safe when works take up part of a road. In Victoria, the rules come from the Code of Practice for Worksite Safety, Traffic Management, made under the Road Management Act 2004. In 2023 the Code adopted the Austroads Guide to Temporary Traffic Management (AGTTM), with Victorian additions from the Department of Transport and Planning (DTP). We design to both.

A construction traffic impact assessment (CTIA) looks wider: how site traffic, truck routes and access affect nearby roads over a project. Councils and DTP often make a CTIA a planning permit condition on bigger jobs. Councils may also ask for a construction management plan, which includes a construction traffic management plan (CTMP). We prepare these across Melbourne metro and regional Victoria.

Consent to work and MoA approvals

Works on a road need the road manager's consent: the council for local roads, DTP for arterial roads. If the works use signs, cones or a temporary speed limit, you also need a Memorandum of Authorisation (MoA). We work out which approvals apply and lodge them for you.

We then design the traffic guidance schemes (TGS), the sign and cone layout drawings. Each one suits the road's speed, traffic volume and sight distance, so your start date holds.

  • Council consent to work or road occupation permits on local roads
  • DTP consent on arterial roads
  • MoA for signs, cones and temporary speed limits
  • We deal with the road manager and lodge on your behalf

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a traffic management plan in Victoria?

Usually, yes, if your works take up or affect a road, footpath or nature strip. That includes lane closures, parking removal and building a crossover. You need the road manager's consent: the council for local roads, DTP for arterial roads. If the works use signs, cones or a temporary speed limit, you also need an MoA.

What's the difference between a TMP and a CTIA?

A TMP is the on-site design to AGTTM, covering signs, devices and lane layouts for the works. A CTIA assesses how construction and heavy-vehicle traffic affect the wider network, usually to satisfy a planning permit condition.

What should a TMP include?

It describes the works, the location, and the dates and hours. It shows how drivers, pedestrians and cyclists will get past safely, using one or more TGS. It also includes a risk assessment and how the setup will be checked and changed. The road manager may ask for more, such as detours or access to nearby properties.

Is a TGS the same as a TMP?

No. A TGS is a drawing of the sign and cone layout for one setup. The TMP is the wider plan for the whole job, and it includes one or more TGS.

What's the difference between a TMP and an MoA?

The TMP is the plan: the documents and TGS showing how the roadworks will run. An MoA authorises the signs, cones and any temporary speed limit on the road. Councils and DTP both issue MoAs. You also need the road manager's consent to do the works.

Who needs to prepare a TMP in Victoria?

On DTP-managed roads, the design must come from a company DTP has accredited for that road category. Councils set their own rules on local roads. TMD categories depend on the road's speed and traffic volume. Category 1 covers simpler, lower-speed roads and Category 3 the high-speed, high-volume end. Fresh Engineering is accredited by DTP for traffic management design.

Can I use a template?

You can start from the standard layouts in AGTTM, but the plan has to fit your site. Speed, traffic volume, sight distance, driveways and footpaths all change the layout. We use standard layouts where they fit and design the rest for your site.

Do I need a TMP for a crossover?

Usually. Building or changing a vehicle crossover (driveway) means working on the footpath, the nature strip and often the road edge. Councils need to give consent first, and many ask for a TMP showing how people walking and driving get past safely. We can prepare it for your application.

How long does approval take?

We can turn the design around in a few business days once we have the scope. Consent and MoA approvals then commonly take one to several weeks, so engage us early.

How much does a traffic management plan cost?

It's driven by the road environment, the number of stages and TGS, and how much work the approvals need. A single-stage closure on a local road is a very different job to multi-stage works on a DTP arterial. Tell us the location and scope and we'll give you a fixed quote.
