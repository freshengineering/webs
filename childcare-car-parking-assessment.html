Parking for a new childcare centre

Most childcare centre applications come down to two parking questions. Is there enough parking for staff, who stay all day? And can parents drop off and pick up children safely, without queuing into the road or filling nearby streets at peak times?

A car parking demand assessment answers both with evidence. It sets the Clause 52.06 requirement against the demand the centre will actually generate, and shows council how the car park and drop-off area will work at the busiest times.

How Clause 52.06 sets childcare parking

Table 1 in Clause 52.06 sets the rate for a child care centre by the number of employees. Since Amendment VC277, the rate depends on the site's Car Parking Requirement map category:

  • Category 1: 1 space to each employee
  • Category 2: 0.5 spaces to each employee
  • Category 3: a minimum of 0.25 spaces to each employee, with no maximum
  • Category 4: no minimum and no maximum

The rate is worked out from staff numbers, not the number of children. Parents still need somewhere to stop at drop-off and pick-up, so council's traffic engineer will usually want to see how that demand is handled too.

A Parking Overlay can set a different rate, so we check the planning scheme for the site first. Our guide explains the VC277 parking categories and when you need a permit to reduce parking.

What council looks for at a childcare centre

  • Staff numbers at the busiest time, including shift overlaps
  • Parent drop-off and pick-up in the morning and late afternoon peaks
  • How long parents stay, and how quickly spaces turn over
  • Whether cars will queue back onto the road at the entry
  • On-street parking near the site, and the effect on nearby homes
  • Safe walking routes from parked cars to the centre entry

Many childcare centres are proposed on residential sites, where council will look closely at the effect on neighbours.

How we assess a childcare centre

We start with how the centre will run: the number of children, staff numbers by shift, opening hours and any sessional programs. Then we estimate demand at the peak and test it against the spaces provided.

  • Staff and parent demand worked out separately
  • Peak drop-off and pick-up demand
  • Surveys of comparable childcare centres where needed
  • On-street parking supply and occupancy at peak times
  • Car park and drop-off layout checked against AS 2890.1 and AS 2890.6
  • Access and queuing at the entry

Traffic as well as parking

A larger centre can also change traffic at the access and nearby intersections. If council asks for a traffic impact assessment, we combine traffic and parking into one report.

Frequently asked questions

How many car parks does a childcare centre need in Victoria?

It depends on the site's Car Parking Requirement map category. Under Clause 52.06, the rate is 1 space to each employee in Category 1, 0.5 in Category 2 and a minimum of 0.25 in Category 3. Category 4 has no minimum. A Parking Overlay can set a different rate, so check the planning scheme for the site.

Can a childcare centre have fewer car parks than Clause 52.06 requires?

Yes, with a permit to reduce the requirement under Clause 52.06-3. Council will want a car parking demand assessment showing that staff and parents can park without unreasonable impact on nearby streets.

Do parents count in the parking rate?

The rate is worked out from staff numbers, but the spaces serve parents too. Council will usually look at drop-off and pick-up peaks as well, so our assessment covers both.

How long does it take?

Usually one to two weeks once plans and the brief are confirmed. It runs longer where surveys are needed, since those have to be done on representative days and avoid school and public holidays.
