What is a road safety audit of temporary traffic management?

Works on live roads change how people drive, walk and ride through a site. A temporary traffic management (TTM) road safety audit, also called a traffic management road safety audit or roadworks road safety audit, is an independent review by accredited road safety auditors. It finds where someone could be killed or seriously injured passing through or working beside the site, and recommends ways to reduce that risk. The Austroads Guide to Road Safety Part 6 (2022) covers it as an audit type of its own.

Our audits are led by an accredited Senior Road Safety Auditor. We're also DTP accredited for traffic management design and hold Traffic Management Designer (TMD) Category 1, 2 & 3, so we know how a traffic guidance scheme is drawn and how it works on the road. As a team, we have 15+ years on road and rail projects. Our project experience includes North East Link, the West Gate Tunnel, Metro Tunnel early works and Level Crossing Removal Package 2.

Two ways we audit traffic management

  • On the drawings: we review the traffic management plan (TMP) and traffic guidance scheme (TGS, the sign and cone layout drawings) before works start, while changes are quick and cheap to make
  • On the road: we inspect the setup under live traffic, by day and at night where the works run after dark, and again when the stage or layout changes

You can book either one, or both. Auditing the drawings first means fewer surprises once the setup goes in.

What the audit looks at

We look at the site the way each road user and worker meets it, from the first warning sign to the end of the works.

  • Advance warning, signs and the speed traffic actually travels through the site
  • Lane closures, merges and tapers, and whether drivers can see and understand them in time
  • Separation between traffic and workers, including safety buffers and how work vehicles get in and out
  • Pedestrian and cyclist routes and detours, including for people with prams or wheelchairs
  • Access to properties, side roads, bus stops and intersections
  • Night conditions: lighting, line marking and how well signs can be seen
  • Changes between stages, where old and new layouts can confuse drivers

A road safety audit is not a compliance check

A road safety audit asks whether the setup is safe, not whether it matches the drawings. A setup can match the approved TGS exactly and still leave a risk the drawings didn't foresee. DTP's road safety audit policy says so directly: an audit of a temporary traffic management setup "is not a check of a TTM set-up's compliance with relevant standards" (DTP, December 2025).

To check that the setup matches the approved TGS, permit and AGTTM, you need a traffic management compliance audit. Many projects need both, and we do both.

DTP sign-off for traffic management audits

For road safety audits commissioned from 19 December 2025 on projects on, or connecting to, DTP roads, DTP has added a review and sign-off step. It covers DTP's own projects, delivery agencies and third parties such as developers (DTP policy amendment).

  • Every completed audit report goes to DTP with the project team's responses, even when every finding is accepted
  • If a finding, its risk rating or the recommended treatment isn't fully accepted, the response must be signed off
  • For audits of temporary traffic management, an executive director at DTP or the delivery agency can sign off
  • The project team lead signs a declaration that they're satisfied with the response and the risk that remains

Allow time for this in your program, and tell us at the quote stage if your project is on a DTP road.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from a traffic management compliance audit?

A compliance audit checks that the setup on the road matches the approved TGS, permit and AGTTM. A road safety audit looks for the crash risk the setup leaves, even when it matches the plan exactly. Many projects need both, and we do both. Read more about our traffic management compliance audits.

When is a road safety audit of traffic management needed?

There's no single rule. Project specifications, DTP and principal contractors commonly ask for one on DTP roads and larger projects. It's often done on the drawings before works start and again once the setup is on the road. A new stage or a big change to the layout is a good time for another.

Do you audit night works?

Yes. Where works run after dark, we inspect the night setup too, because lighting, line marking and how well signs can be seen all change at night.

How fast is the report?

Fast. Usually a few business days after the inspection. Anything critical we raise with your site team on site, straight away.

Can you audit traffic management you designed?

No. The audit team must be independent of the designers, so we don't audit our own traffic management designs. If we designed your traffic management, we can recommend another auditor.
