Traffic impact assessments for planning permits

A traffic impact assessment (TIA) is the traffic report for a planning permit. Some councils call it a traffic engineering assessment. Councils and the Department of Transport and Planning (DTP) ask for one to check that your development can be reached and parked safely, without overloading nearby roads. It gives the responsible authority the evidence it needs to grant the permit.

Our project experience ranges from infill subdivisions to North East Link and Level Crossing Removal Package 2. The aim is a clear report that answers the council traffic engineer's questions before they're asked. A request for further information can restart the permit clock and add weeks, so we'd rather deal with it in the first report.

Traffic impact assessments at a glance

  • Permit-ready report for council and DTP
  • Intersection modelling in SIDRA software
  • Access, sight distance and swept paths
  • Parking assessed to Clause 52.06 and AS 2890
  • Answers to council and DTP questions

When a planning permit needs a traffic impact assessment

Most permits for higher-traffic uses trigger one, and your council will usually flag it at the pre-application stage. Talk to us early and we'll confirm scope before any work starts.

  • Service stations, childcare, supermarkets, drive-throughs
  • Subdivisions and multi-unit residential
  • Industrial, warehousing and freight uses
  • Any new or altered access to a declared road
  • Car parking shortfalls under Clause 52.06

If the development builds roads or intersections that go to council or connect to a DTP road, the permit commonly asks for a road safety audit too.

How we assess traffic impact (SIDRA modelling and access)

We scale the analysis to the proposal, so a small site isn't carrying the workload of a regional centre. Where access touches a declared road, DTP is a referral authority and we'll set scope to suit them up front.

  • Confirm permit triggers and referral requirements
  • Survey existing conditions and check crash history
  • SIDRA intersection modelling for the design year
  • Access, sight distance and swept paths to Austroads
  • Movement and Place context where it applies

What's in our traffic impact assessment report

A lodgement-ready report written for the decision-maker, not buried in model output. We stay on after lodgement to answer council or DTP requests for further information.

  • Permit-ready TIA with documented assumptions
  • Intersection results: how busy each intersection gets, delays and queues
  • Parking and loading against Clause 52.06 and AS 2890
  • Practical treatment recommendations

What we need from you

Send what you have. We'll tell you if anything is missing.

  • Site address
  • Development plans, including the site plan and access points
  • The proposed use, with floor areas or the number of dwellings or lots
  • Operating hours, staff and visitor numbers, and delivery or waste vehicle needs
  • Any existing traffic counts or earlier traffic reports for the site
  • Council or DTP correspondence, such as pre-application advice or a request for further information

What affects the cost of a traffic impact assessment

There's no set price, because no two sites are the same. These are the things that change the scope:

  • The size and type of development
  • Whether new traffic counts are needed, and at how many locations
  • How many intersections need SIDRA modelling
  • Access to a declared road, which brings in DTP as a referral authority
  • Swept path checks for larger vehicles, such as delivery and waste trucks

Recent counts or an earlier traffic report for the site can cut the work. Send us the plans and we'll scope and quote it.

Frequently asked questions

When do I need a TIA for a planning permit in Victoria?

Generally when a development adds notable traffic, changes site access, or accesses a declared road where DTP is a referral authority. Your council will confirm it at pre-application, or we can advise on your site.

How long does one take?

Usually two to three weeks once we have plans and surveys. New traffic counts add lead time since they must avoid school holidays and atypical conditions.

Will council accept the report?

No consultant can promise approval. What we can do is use recognised Austroads methods and set out every assumption, so council and DTP can test the report. If they ask for further information, we answer it.

What's the difference between a traffic impact and a transport impact assessment?

A traffic impact assessment focuses on vehicle traffic, site access and parking. A transport impact assessment looks wider, at walking, cycling, public transport and freight as well. The names are often used loosely, so we scope the report to cover what council or DTP asks for.

When does DTP get involved?

DTP gets involved when a development needs a permit to create or change access to a road in a Transport Zone 2. That zone is used for main roads that DTP manages, and council refers these applications to DTP. Residential developments of 60 or more dwellings or lots are also referred to DTP. Where DTP is involved, we set the scope to suit it from the start.

How much does a traffic impact assessment cost?

It scales with the proposal. A small infill development with simple access costs far less than a service station on a declared road with SIDRA modelling and DTP referral. Traffic counts, if new ones are needed, are the other variable. Send us the plans and we'll scope and quote it.

Do I need new traffic counts?

Not always. Where recent, representative data exists we use it. At traffic lights we often start from the detector counts in Trafficeye, our traffic signal volume tool. New counts are needed when the network has changed, or when council or DTP asks for current data. They have to be collected on typical days, outside school and public holidays, which is what adds lead time.

Can the TIA cover car parking as well?

Yes. Most of our TIAs include the Clause 52.06 parking assessment and an AS 2890 layout check in the same report. Where you're seeking a car parking reduction, we can add the demand assessment too. One combined report is usually easier for the assessing planner.
