Parking for a new gym or studio

Gyms and fitness studios often open in warehouse units, shopfronts and mixed-use buildings where parking is shared or limited. Council will want to know there's enough parking at the busiest class times, especially when one class arrives before the last one has left.

Clause 52.06 doesn't set a rate for most fitness uses, so the number of spaces is up to council. A car parking demand assessment gives council a clear figure, based on how the studio will run.

Why gyms and studios need a parking assessment

Table 1 in Clause 52.06 doesn't list gyms or fitness studios. Councils usually treat them as an indoor recreation facility, which has no set rate. Under Clause 52.06-6, parking for a use not in Table 1 must be provided to the satisfaction of the responsible authority.

That leaves council to decide how many spaces are enough, and council's traffic engineer will usually ask for evidence. The use term council applies matters, so we check it first. A Parking Overlay can also set its own rate in some areas.

What drives parking demand at a fitness studio

  • Maximum class size, such as the number of reformers or bikes
  • The class timetable, and the overlap when one class arrives as another leaves
  • Peak times, often early morning, evening and weekends
  • Instructor and staff numbers
  • Gym floor capacity and member numbers, for an open gym
  • Walking, cycling and public transport access for members

Peak times at a studio often differ from neighbouring businesses. In a warehouse estate or shopping strip, that can mean spaces are free when the studio needs them most.

How we assess a gym or studio

We start with how the studio will run: class sizes, the timetable, staff numbers and opening hours. Then we work out demand at the busiest changeover and test it against the spaces available.

  • Demand worked out from class sizes and the timetable
  • Changeover overlap tested at the busiest class times
  • Surveys of comparable studios where needed
  • Parking shared with other tenants, by time of day
  • On-street parking supply and occupancy at peak times
  • Layout checked against AS 2890.1

Council may tie the permit to a maximum class size or number of people on site. We base the assessment on the numbers you plan to run, so the permit fits how you'll operate.

Frequently asked questions

How many car parks does a gym need in Victoria?

Clause 52.06 doesn't set a rate for most gyms and fitness studios. Parking must be provided to the satisfaction of council, which usually asks for a car parking demand assessment based on class sizes, the timetable and peak times. A Parking Overlay may set its own rate in some areas.

Does a small pilates or yoga studio need a parking assessment?

Often, if council asks how parking will work. Even a small studio can create a peak when classes change over. For a small studio, a short parking letter is sometimes enough. We'll tell you which applies before quoting.

Can a gym share parking with other businesses?

Often, yes. Studios tend to be busiest early morning, evening and weekends, when many neighbouring businesses are quiet. The assessment can show council that shared spaces will be free when the studio needs them.

How long does it take?

Usually one to two weeks once plans and the brief are confirmed. It runs longer where surveys are needed, since those have to be done on representative days and avoid school and public holidays.
